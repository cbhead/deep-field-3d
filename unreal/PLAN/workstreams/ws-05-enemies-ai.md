## Open questions
- **The downhill threshold is a reading, not a quotation.** B§1.5 and §3.2 both say "uphill ×0.85 above 15 % grade, downhill ×1.1" and neither says what grade *downhill* starts at. `FDFLaneWalker::SlopeFactor` reads it symmetrically — flat walking between −15 % and +15 %, so a gentle descent is not a free 10 % — because the alternative (any negative grade pays ×1.1) makes almost every lane faster than its authored length and quietly shortens every wave. The three numbers are `FDFLaneWalkerParams` fields, not literals, so WS-27 can settle it with a dial rather than a code change; `slopeGradeThresholdPercent` would be the name and 15 the value. Flagging rather than assuming: the other reading is defensible and it is a balance call, not mine.
- **A stranded walker reports cut off — settled, and it is parity rather than judgement.** INT found the ground I had missed: the sim already does exactly this (`World.cs:116`, infinity mapped to `float.MaxValue` "so it sorts last rather than first"). More importantly it found that my condition and the sim's are *not the same condition* and only coincide while siege routing works — the sim's is a severed **position** (the node ahead reaches no core), mine was a severed **step** (routing found nothing here). Fixed in `961504a`: siege routing ported, so a `StructureDps > 0` walker is priced through shut edges and never strands at a wall. **WS-04, the two things targeting must know:** the metric is an opaque sort key (never do arithmetic on it — float max plus anything is float max), and a sieging enemy is not stranded and needs no special case.
- ~~**A stranded walker reports cut off; the graph's own metric cannot.**~~ `UDFLaneGraphAsset::RemainingToCore` prices an itinerary's remaining edges without asking whether they are open — deliberately, and C6 says why ("a closed edge later on the plan is the tick's problem, not this metric's"). But a stranded walker has already asked the tick and been told there is nowhere to go, so `FDFLaneWalker::RemainingToCoreMeters` returns the cut-off sentinel for it. WS-04: this is what stops a wave held at a shut gate from reading as the closest thing to the core and pulling every tower off the enemies actually walking in. If targeting would rather see the real distance and check `bStranded` itself, say so — it is a one-line change and I would rather it be your call than my default.
- **Who owns the match phase.** The director owns *what spawns when* and *when the wave is over*; it does not own intermission, lives, victory/defeat, and it does not broadcast `DF.Message.Wave*` — it hands the payload out via `DescribeWave` and lets the phase owner send it. WS-?? (DFMatch, currently WS-00's skeleton): confirm the phase machine is yours and that `OnWaveCleared` → intermission → `BeginWave` is the shape you want, or say what you would rather call.
- **Counting bodies the plan did not create.** `NotifyEnemyAdded/Removed` is how a wave knows it is clear. Split children (cluster → 5 motes) and brood vents are *added*; every death and leak is *removed*. WS-05's own spawner will call these, but WS-19's boss vents and WS-24's mutables create bodies too — anything that spawns an enemy outside the plan must tell the director or a wave will clear with enemies alive.
- **Where the RNG streams live.** `DFDetRng.h` / `DFDetMath.h` are header-only with no DF dependencies and sit in DFEnemies for now. WS-09's condition `HazardStream` (B§2.10) and WS-06's scrap need the same streams and cannot depend on DFEnemies (sibling / lower layer). Proposal: a `contract-append` moving both headers to `Source/DFCore/Public/Determinism/` when the second consumer shows up; nothing else changes.
- **Float determinism on Game targets.** UBT (5.8, BuildSettings V7) compiles Editor targets with precise FP but leaves Game/Client/Server at Default, which is `/fp:fast` on MSVC. Resume-under-a-new-host re-plans the current wave on another machine, so the plan must not depend on it: the two headers and `DFWavePlan.cpp` pin their arithmetic with pragmas (`float_control(precise)` on MSVC, `clang fp contract(off) reassociate(off)`). Unverified on Windows until the GPU box exists.
- **Route ids — answered 2026-09-21 by WS-09's landing.** Every `routeId` the five wave tables name has a matching `FDFLaneItinerary::Id` in the imported level files (foundry `air/ground`; switchyard `+groundShort`; spire `air/escape/stair`; toaster `air/direct/long/west`; testlane `ground`) — checked, not assumed. So `FDFSpawnEntry::RouteId` resolves with `FindItinerary` and the spawner walks `EdgesOf(Itinerary)`: **a route is its itinerary, never a shortest path** — "the long way round" is the point of naming one (INT, 2026-09-21), and re-planning happens only at a junction via `ChooseEdge`. Still open for the WS-10x redesigns: a map that renames a route must rename it in `waves_<map>.json` in the same PR, or the director will refuse the wave. Worth a `DF.Map.Validate` rule (WS-09) rather than a runtime error.
- **Sim rules kept as they are, for WS-27 to know about:** (1) a stealth-weighted condition adds `round(authoredCount × (factor − 1))` bodies — off the authored count, not the player-scaled one, and ties round to even (5 shades at night → +2, not +3); (2) player scaling multiplies every group, including one authored with an `Elite` — `EliteCap` is WS-17's to enforce; (3) the sim's `List.Sort` is unstable, so where entries tie on (tick, def id) the sim's order is a .NET accident — here ties keep plan order.
- **The baseline file.** `docs/gate-baseline.tsv` has no hp column, and its `spawned` is plan bodies + 5 per killed Cluster. `foundry/4p` (203) is a two-player match (its ember/forge/ember/forge party has two factions refused); four seated players is the `~not-a-gate` row (356). `DF.Unit.WavePlanBaseline` reads live tables, so the first rebalance of `waves_*.json` or the growth dials fails it on purpose — re-baseline or retire it at G3.
